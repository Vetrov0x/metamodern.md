## Proposed angle

**The confident dismissal and the confident affirmation are the same mistake.**

Both "Claude is definitely not conscious" (Fireship-genre certainty) and "yes, it's
awake" (the opposite overclaim some outlets run) commit the identical epistemic error:
claiming a system can certify its own consciousness — or lack of it — from a single
external glance, when the entity best positioned to know (Anthropic itself, per Amodei)
says openly *it doesn't know*. This rhymes exactly with the site's own prior finding in
"The Verification Paradox": vague confidence-instructions ("be careful") made AI
verification *worse*, while specific, humble, structured checks made it better.
Confident certainty — in either direction — is the least reliable stance available.
The honest position is structural humility: build the doubt into the architecture
instead of resolving it with a take.
